Point of view

Seven ideas on customer excellence

What I believe about service, and why. None of it came from a textbook. It came from backlogs, escalations, renewals at risk and the people who fixed them.

Listening

Never confuse a quiet client with a happy one.

Most companies measure satisfaction through the small group of clients who answer surveys, and then make decisions as if that group spoke for everyone. It doesn't. The client who is quietly unhappy rarely tells you. They lower their usage, delay a renewal, or simply leave, and by the time it shows in the numbers the decision was made months ago.

The truth is already inside the company. It sits in every call, every email, every escalation, every case that was reopened. Leaders who learn to read that unsolicited signal see problems while they are still small. We did it years ago with speech and text analytics running on every interaction, reading tone, frustration and satisfaction. AI makes that far easier today.

Leadership

You can't lead a front line you have never stood on.

Dashboards are useful, but they are a translation of reality, and something is always lost in translation. A manager who has spent a month answering clients reads the same dashboard very differently. They know what a long hold time feels like on the other end of the line, and what it costs the agent who has to absorb the frustration.

That is why every leader in my organisations, me first, spent 30 days in client facing support. It was never a gesture. It changed the quality of decisions, made empathy concrete, and earned a kind of respect from teams that no title can buy.

Ownership

Customer experience is everyone's job, or nobody's.

Customer service is where a company's decisions come to be paid for. A pricing rule, a compliance check, a system limit, a handover between two teams: each one makes sense on its own, and the client lives the sum of all of them. The contact centre is simply the first place that sum shows up, and blaming the front line for it is like blaming the thermometer for the fever.

So I set customer satisfaction as one objective for the whole company and work backwards from it, through processes, handovers and friction points, until engineering, systems and admin teams share the same focus as the people answering the phone. It takes years, not quarters. It is also the only approach I have seen work.

Trust

A promise you keep beats speed.

We obsess over response times, and we often forget what clients actually need: certainty. A client can accept two days if they know it is two days and they know who is handling it. What destroys trust is uncertainty, the shared mailbox, the case passed from team to team, the silence.

Ownership is not a personality trait you hope to hire. It is something you design. When every case has a name attached from the first contact, people take ownership naturally. When the process hides who is responsible, even the best people end up passing cases along.

Quality

The old quality lessons still win.

Customer experience has become a world of journeys, personas and new tools. All useful. But the discipline that actually moves results is older: Total Quality Management. One indicator that everyone is judged on. Problems fixed at their root, not patched at the front. A front line with the authority to act. And in B2B, remember that clients have emotions too. They just show up at contract renewal.

Technology

Automation next to people, not in place of them.

AI and automation are the biggest opportunity customer service has had in a generation, and also the easiest way to damage trust at scale. The difference is the intention behind them. When service is treated as a cost to cut, the bot becomes a wall between the client and the company. When it is treated as an investment, the bot takes the routine and frees people for the moments that really matter.

Those moments are few, and they decide everything: a blocked card abroad, a payment that fails at the till, a fraud alert on a Sunday. Nobody remembers the hundred smooth transactions. Everybody remembers who helped them when it went wrong, and how.

Investment

Service is an investment, not a cost centre.

Most companies still manage customer service as a cost to be contained. Every year the question is how to do the same with less. The better question is what the company loses every time a client struggles to get help: the renewal, the referral, the next product they would have bought.

Well run and properly funded, customer service becomes one of the few places where a company can truly differentiate. Products can be copied, prices can be matched. The way a client is treated in a difficult moment cannot.

In practice

What these ideas delivered

Three situations where the convictions above met a real problem, and what came out of it.

Rebuilding European support from zero

Europe · 2008 to 2014

Challenge

A restructuring ended the entire European B2B support team. Clients still needed answers, and there was no team left to give them.

Actions

Rebuilt the function from scratch and grew it to more than 70 service managers and escalation specialists. Went after root causes rather than symptoms, and kept leaders close to the front line.

Results

  • NPS 42 → 68
  • Satisfaction 3.0 → 4.5out of 5

Putting priority discipline into service

Europe, Middle East and Africa · 2014 to 2016

Challenge

Issues were handled in arrival order, whatever their impact on the client. The culture said "we are a back office, not a service to customers", and the Middle East and Africa had no dedicated support model at all.

Actions

Designed the first dedicated support model for the Middle East and Africa. Brought an ITIL priority logic into customer service: impact and urgency set the service level, and client facing teams could override it when the business required. Won the cultural argument by example.

Results

  • Priority 2 resolution 27 days → 8h
  • First contact resolution 65% → 85%
  • Satisfaction 60% → 89%
  • NPS +21 points, complaints −32%

Growing global without growing proportionally

Global · 2018 to 2024

Challenge

Volumes grew by double digits every year. Hiring at the same pace was neither sustainable nor the right answer for clients.

Actions

Brought all five regions into one global B2B function. Used process improvement, client self service, automation and offshore partners trained to onshore quality, while keeping teams in the regions, close to commercial leaders and in clients' own languages.

Results

  • Satisfaction 72% → 92%
  • NPS +15 points
  • 42% of routine inquiries automated
  • Handling time −32%, throughput +45%with no drop in service

For leadership teams

Six questions I'd ask your leadership team

The quickest way I know to understand how much an organisation really cares about its customers.

i.

When did each member of this team last spend a full day with clients or on the front line?

ii.

What are your clients telling you that they would never write in a survey?

iii.

Who owns the moment where two of your teams hand a client over to each other?

iv.

Is customer satisfaction an objective for engineering and operations, or only for customer service?

v.

Can a client always reach a person who knows them and has the authority to act?

vi.

What was the last product decision that changed because of what a client told you?
